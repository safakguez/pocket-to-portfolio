Pocket to Portfolio in the Classroom

Teacher guide · personal finance · ages 13+ · one 90-minute session

What is it?

Pocket to Portfolio is a free game for smartphones and tablets (Android and iOS). Students start at 15 with $20 of pocket money a month, living with their parents. They earn their first money with side jobs, choose an apprenticeship or college, move out and decide what their money does. They live with the consequences of their decisions without risking real money. At the end they get a life summary.

Watch the 75-second preview video

  • Subjects: personal finance, economics, business, career and life skills, math (percentages and interest)
  • Age: 13 and up (grade 8 and above), high school and vocational education
  • Time: one 90-minute session (or two 45-minute periods), optional homework and follow-up lesson
  • Devices: students' phones or tablets (Android 7.0+, iOS 15+); working in pairs is fine

Learning goals

Students …

  • tell gross pay from net pay and plan a monthly budget with fixed costs (rent to their parents, rent, living costs),
  • explain why an emergency fund of about three months of expenses comes before investing,
  • describe how compound interest works in a regular ETF savings plan and compare it with a savings account,
  • spot cost traps: overdraft interest, buying on installments, lifestyle creep, expensive funds, Ponzi schemes,
  • evaluate decisions under uncertainty, for example during a market crash: hold or sell?

Preparation

  • Have students install the app beforehand: Google Play · App Store. There is no account and no sign-up. The game also works offline.
  • Game time: one game month takes about one minute. In a 90-minute session students play roughly from age 15 to 20.
  • Currency: students can choose $, €, £ or CHF. It is display only – the amounts are the same everywhere.
  • Ad-free in class: the game is funded by non-personalized ads (a short ad break every two game years, optional reward videos) and an optional one-time purchase, “Gold” (no subscription), that removes the ad breaks. There is no in-game currency, no sponsorship by banks, brokers or financial providers and no affiliate links. For school classes we send free codes for “Gold”, so there are no ad breaks. Lesson plan (90 minutes)

1. Warm-up (10 minutes)

Ask the class: “You get $20 of pocket money a month. What do you do with it?” Collect the answers on the board and sort them: spend, save, invest.

2. Getting started (5 minutes)

Open the app, choose a character, name and currency. Show briefly: the “Work” tab (side jobs, energy), the “Bank” tab (checking account, savings account, world ETF savings plan, money quiz) and the life score at the top.

3. Play phase 1 (30 minutes)

Task: “Earn your first money, build an emergency fund and start an ETF savings plan. Answer the quiz questions in the Bank tab.” The monthly reports show income and expenses.

4. Check-in (10 minutes)

Pair work: compare checking account, savings account and investments. Who already has a savings plan? How big is the emergency fund? What cost money without being necessary?

5. Play phase 2 (20 minutes)

Keep playing until training, college or moving out. Watch the monthly report: how do gross pay, net pay, rent to your parents and rent change?

6. Debrief (15 minutes)

Use the discussion questions below and compare life scores. What matters is not who has the most, but why.

Discussion questions

  1. Why is an overdraft so expensive? How did you avoid it in the game?
  2. What difference does it make to start a savings plan at 16 instead of 26? The compound interest cards in the game help.
  3. A friend promises “20% returns a month”. How do you recognize a Ponzi scheme?
  4. The market drops 30%. Sell or hold – what speaks for each?
  5. What does “financially free” mean? When does the income from your investments cover your expenses?

Going further

As homework, keep playing until age 25 and write down your net worth. In the follow-up lesson, compare: what role do the savings rate, education, lifestyle and the timing of the savings plan play? Students who want to can sign in with Google Play Games or Game Center and see their result on the worldwide leaderboard “Net worth at 25”. This is optional.
